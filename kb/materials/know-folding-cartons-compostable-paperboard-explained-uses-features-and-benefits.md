## Article

- Materials have become a significant factor as brands are looking for new ways to improve their product packaging. And compostable paperboard is one of those new options that has gained popularity due to its decomposable quality.
- But what exactly makes paperboard compostable? Where can it be used? And what should brands know before choosing it? Let's take a simple look.

## What Is a Compostable Paperboard?

- It is a paper-based material designed to break down into natural materials under the right composting conditions.
- It is usually made from paper fibers and might include coatings, inks, or other treatments. These additional factors matter a lot because they can affect whether the finished package is suitable for composting or not.
- This is why not every paperboard box should automatically be called compostable. The full packaging structure needs to meet the requirements for the intended composting system.

## What Makes It Different?

- The primary difference between compostable packaging and other materials is what becomes of it after it is no longer required.
- Regular paperboard can break down over time, but that does not mean it is suitable for composting. Whereas compostable paperboard is suitable for this process.
- The breaking down of this material highly depends on coating, inks, adhesives, thickness, and the composting environment.
- This makes the complete packaging design important, not just the paperboard itself.

## What Should Brands Check Before Choosing It?

- The word "compostable" should not be the only thing brands look at.
- They should check how the complete package is made and what type of composting environment it is designed for. Commercial composting and home composting can have different requirements.
- Brands should also consider printing, coatings, adhesives, product contact, moisture resistance, and the strength needed for shipping and storage.
- Clear material information can help prevent confusion for both brands and customers.
