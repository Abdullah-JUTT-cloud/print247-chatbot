## Article

- A customer recently asked us why their product labels are not sticking well with the bottles. Stickers lift at the corners within a few days of hitting a warehouse shelf. It turned out that the adhesive material for labels wasn't compatible with the product's surface. Most of the time, it's the result of one of a few decisions made before the order is printed. You should opt for peelable adhesive when you need a label to hold strong and then come off clean.

## What Makes an Adhesive "Peelable" Or Permanent

- A peelable or removable adhesive is a special type of glue applied to tags and stickers. When you pull it cleanly, it will come out of your product or shipping box securely. There are no sticky marks left behind, unlike permanent glue. These easily removable labels are ideal for temporary sales and inventory tracking.

## Avoid These Mistakes When Ordering Labels With Peelable Adhesive

- The process of ordering labels with peelable adhesive might seem simple to you. It is important to keep in mind, however, that small errors can bog down your production process.
- Here are mistakes business owners make along with tips on how to avoid them.

## Neglecting the Surface Texture And Material

- Brands make the biggest mistake by forgetting what the label represents. Smooth glass is very different from rough cardboard or soft plastic when it comes to peelable adhesives. If your packaging is bumpy - the glue might not stick properly. Certain plastics may become permanent if peelable labels are applied to them over time. Be sure to tell your printing partner which material you are using for your tags.

## Ignoring Environmental Exposure and Temperature

- Its up to you whether you make peelable adhesive work better or worse for you. If your items sit in a freezing warehouse or ride in a hot delivery truck - the glue changes. High heat can melt the backing that leaves a sticky mess on your retail boxes. Cold air can freeze the glue that makes your tags fall right off.

## Skipping Real-World Prototype Testing

- If you intend to purchase thousands of labels with peelable adhesive, you should test them first. A glue may sound perfect on paper, but fails in your warehouse. Request a physical sample from your label supplier. Put the label on your own product package, keep it in your own storeroom for a few days and then check if you can take off that label. This is a simple experiment to prove the effectiveness of the tag and save your money.

## Miscalculating the Dwell Time Before Removal

- Dwell time for labels simply means how much time does the label remain on your product before peeling it off. Six month performance of stickers made from peelable adhesive will be equal to the performance on the first day. This is not the case; with the increase in the dwell time, it becomes more difficult to peel off. It will require a very specialized backing to leave the tags on for an entire year.

## Picking the Wrong Label Material for Clean Peeling

- The glue is only one part of the story. It is just as important to pay attention to the top layer of your tag. Choosing cheap paper for peelable labels will likely result in the paper tearing right down the middle. This defeats the whole purpose of a clean peel. Instead, you should choose strong materials like vinyl or poly film. By using these tough options, you can remove the entire tag in one piece without tearing or ripping.
