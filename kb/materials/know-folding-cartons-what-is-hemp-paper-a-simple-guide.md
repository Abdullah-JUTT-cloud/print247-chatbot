## Article

- When finding suitable packaging material options that are safe for the environment and sustainable, you might have come across hemp paper. It might sound like a modern stock but it has a long history in paper materials. In this article, we'll explain what it is, how it's made, and why it matters.

## What Is Hemp Paper?

- Hemp paper, as the name suggests, is made from hemp plants. Unlike traditional paper made from wood pulp. Hemp is a variety of Cannabis sativa; industrial hemp contains no more than 0.3% THC.s. It's grown specifically for its strong and fibrous stalks.

## A Little History

- Hemp paper has been in use for centuries. The earliest known paper, made in China more than 2,000 years ago, used hemp fibers. In Europe, it was also a popular choice for usage in books and maps before wood pulp paper became a standard in the 1800s.

## How Is Hemp Paper Made?

- The process is similar to traditional papermaking but there are a few differences:.
- Harvesting & decortication - Hemp stalks are harvested and the outer bark is separated from the woody core.
- Fiber extraction - The bast fibers are pulled out and processed.

## Pulping - These fibers are then broken down into pulp

- Sheet formation - The pulp is formed into sheets, then pressed and dried.

## Why Hemp Paper Stands Out

- Hemp matures in about 3-4 months. That's far faster than the 7-20+ years it takes for trees to grow. This makes it a far more renewable resource.
- Hemp can give more usable fiber per acre than trees. It means less land area is needed for the same amount of paper.
- Hemp fibers are longer and tougher than wood fibers. This makes this paper more tear-resistant and long-lasting than regular paper.
- Hemp contains less lignin than trees so its bleaching is easy.
