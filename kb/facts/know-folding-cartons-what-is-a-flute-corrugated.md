## Understanding the Structure of A-Flute Corrugated Board

- Like other corrugated materials, A-Flute consists of flat liner sheets with a corrugated (fluted) panel placed between them.
- The key difference between A-flute and other flute options is the size of the corrugation.
- A-Flute contains larger and wider flutes as compared to B-Flute, E-Flute, or F-Flute. These larger waves give more air pockets within the board structure.
- When a corrugated box comes in contact with shipping and impacts of rough handling conditions, the A-flutes act as a shield between them and the product.
- This design makes A-Flute one of the best choices when protection is the top priority.

## How A-Flute Differs From Other Flute Options

- Each corrugated flute profile serves a different purpose.
- F-Flute and E-Flute are most commonly used for retail packaging because they offer a thin profile and better print results.
- B-Flute focuses on crush resistance and efficient storage.
- C-Flute provides a balanced combination of cushioning and strength.
- A-Flute is designed primarily for shock absorption and product protection.
- If your goal is maximum cushioning rather than compact packaging, A-Flute is often the stronger choice.
- The drawback of choosing this stock is that the packaging becomes thick.
